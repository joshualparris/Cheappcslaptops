# Dubbo Refurbished Laptop Market — Viability Research

**Research date:** 23 September 2026  
**Market:** Dubbo / Dubbo Regional NSW  
**Question:** Is there enough local demand to profitably buy cheap old laptops, refurbish or upgrade them, and resell them?

## Bottom line

There appears to be a **real local market for affordable refurbished laptops**, but the strongest opportunity is not “any old laptop with an SSD”.

The most promising resale lane is:

- business-class Dell Latitude / HP EliteBook or ProBook / Lenovo ThinkPad
- preferably **officially Windows 11-supported hardware**
- normally 8th-gen Intel Core or a comparable supported Ryzen platform
- 8 GB RAM minimum
- 256 GB SSD preferred
- healthy battery
- known-good charger
- fully tested and cleaned
- sold locally with transparent condition and test results

A likely useful retail band for Dubbo is approximately **$150–$250**, with better 10th-gen-or-newer systems extending higher.

This is a hypothesis supported by current market evidence, not a guarantee of sale volume. Facebook Marketplace does not expose reliable completed-sale data publicly, so the best next step is a controlled local test listing.

## Why Dubbo looks viable

### 1. There is already a local refurbished-computer market

Leading Edge Computers Dubbo explicitly advertises **refurbished and new computers**, along with local support and warranties.

Source:
- https://dubbochamber.com.au/chamber-members/leading-edge-computers-dubbo

This is useful evidence because an established local computer business is already serving the category rather than treating refurbished computers as a purely online niche.

### 2. Dubbo is large enough to support a budget tier

The 2021 Census recorded **54,922 people in Dubbo Regional**, with a median age of **36** and median weekly household income of **$1,597**.

Source:
- https://www.abs.gov.au/census/find-census-data/quickstats/2021/LGA12390

For Dubbo locality itself, the ABS recorded **43,516 people**, median age **35**, and median household income **$1,690/week**, compared with **$1,829/week across NSW**.

Source:
- https://www.abs.gov.au/census/find-census-data/quickstats/2021/SAL11299

That does not prove laptop demand by itself, but it supports the existence of a sizeable family, student, home-computing and price-conscious customer base.

## The important 2026 hardware cutoff

Windows 10 support ended on **14 October 2025**. Microsoft recommends moving eligible systems to Windows 11, using Extended Security Updates temporarily, or replacing unsupported devices.

Sources:
- https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/deployment/updates-lifecycle/windows-10-support-has-ended-on-october-14-2025
- https://www.microsoft.com/windows/end-of-support

That changes the resale value of older DadLAN-style machines.

Older 4th–7th-gen Intel laptops can still be useful machines, but ordinary buyers now have a stronger reason to prefer devices that are officially supported by Windows 11.

### Recommended normal resale floor

For stock bought specifically to resell:

- **Prefer:** Intel 8th gen or newer, or comparable officially supported Ryzen hardware
- **Accept older stock only when:** acquisition cost is extremely low, the intended use is clear, or Linux / specialist use is appropriate
- **Avoid paying normal resale-stock prices for:** old Celerons, 4th-gen systems, and 6th/7th-gen Core machines that need substantial parts

## Where the margin is created

The profit is mostly created **when buying the machine**, not by installing Windows.

RAM, SSDs, batteries and chargers can quickly erase a margin. A laptop bought cheaply but missing several parts can be worse than a more expensive complete system.

### Working sourcing model

Indicative targets for evaluating purchases:

| Target machine | Maximum sensible all-in stock cost | Indicative local asking range | Indicative gross room before overhead/time |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 8th-gen i5, 8 GB, 256 GB SSD | $100–$120 | $190–$240 | $70–$140 |
| 10th-gen i5, 8 GB, 256 GB SSD | $130–$150 | $230–$300 | $80–$170 |
| 6th/7th-gen i5, 8 GB, SSD | $40–$60 | $100–$150 | $40–$110 |
| 4th-gen / old Celeron | free–$30 preferred | $50–$100 | small |
| Dead/untested repair project | $10–$40 | highly variable | high risk / potentially high return |

These are **buying-decision ranges**, not guaranteed Dubbo sale prices.

## Recommended buy rule

For a laptop purchased specifically to flip:

> **Do not buy it unless there is roughly $80–$100 of visible gross margin after all known parts, delivery and repair costs.**

The buffer is needed for:

- hidden faults
- dead or weak batteries
- missing chargers
- replacement keyboards
- bad SSDs
- cleaning materials
- failed repair attempts
- buyer negotiation
- returns / consumer obligations
- time spent testing and preparing the system

## The product that is easiest to sell

A useful local listing would look more like this:

> **HP EliteBook / Dell Latitude — $199**  
> Intel Core i5 · 8 GB RAM · 256 GB SSD · Windows 11  
> Fully cleaned and tested. Genuine charger included.  
> Battery health and SSD health checked. Wi-Fi, webcam, speakers, keyboard and USB ports tested.  
> Local Dubbo pickup — buyer can test it before purchase.

The differentiator is not merely “SSD installed”.

The value proposition is:

- local
- cheaper than many professional refurbishers
- transparent
- clean
- tested
- ready to use
- buyer can inspect it
- seller can explain exactly what was upgraded

## Local positioning

Do not try to beat large professional refurbishers on long warranties or scale.

A more realistic position is:

**Affordable + local + transparent + tested + approachable**

Useful buyer groups may include:

- families needing a second computer
- high-school and tertiary students
- people replacing a failed home laptop
- seniors needing a basic computer
- job seekers
- households needing a basic Office/browser/video-call machine
- small community organisations
- buyers who do not want to spend $500+ on a new laptop

## NSW second-hand dealer requirements

This is a material cost and process issue.

NSW Government guidance says a second-hand dealer licence is required to run a business buying, selling or exchanging prescribed used goods. **Electronic goods are prescribed second-hand goods.**

Sources:
- https://www.nsw.gov.au/business-and-economy/running-a-business/industry-specific-business-requirements/pawnbrokers-and-second-hand-dealers/pawnbroker-and-second-hand-dealer-licences
- https://www.nsw.gov.au/business-and-economy/running-a-business/industry-specific-business-requirements/pawnbrokers-and-second-hand-dealers

For the **2026–27 financial year**, the application fee for a new 1-year pawnbroker or second-hand dealer licence is **$692**.

Source:
- https://www.nsw.gov.au/business-and-economy/running-a-business/industry-specific-business-requirements/pawnbrokers-and-second-hand-dealers/pawnbrokers-and-second-hand-dealers-fees

Current NSW guidance also says licensees generally need to:

- check supplier identity and obtain an ownership statement
- keep transaction records electronically
- retain transaction records for three years
- transmit acquisition records electronically to NSW Police within three days
- hold regulated second-hand goods for **14 days** before selling them
- not alter those goods during the 14-day retention period
- notify Fair Trading of regular business/storage premises

There are exemptions and special cases, so official guidance should be checked before trading.

### Why the licence changes the economics

A $692 first-year licence contributes approximately:

| Units sold in first year | Licence cost per unit |
| ---: | ---: |
| 5 | $138.40 |
| 10 | $69.20 |
| 20 | $34.60 |
| 30 | $23.07 |
| 50 | $13.84 |

That makes very-low-margin flipping unattractive.

The business makes more sense when:

- sourcing is excellent
- gross margin is healthy
- enough units are sold to spread fixed compliance costs
- failure/return rates are kept low

## Practical conclusion

### Is there a market?

**Probably yes.** There is evidence of an established local refurbished-computer category and Dubbo is large enough to support a lower-cost computing tier.

### Can refurbished laptops make a profit?

**Potentially, yes — but only with disciplined sourcing.** Paying $80–$100 for old unsupported laptops and then adding expensive parts is unlikely to be a reliable business.

The more attractive model is buying desirable business-class laptops cheaply enough that the completed machine still has a large margin.

### Best initial experiment

Before buying bulk stock:

1. Pick one laptop already owned and genuinely surplus.
2. Refurbish it to the standard intended for future sales.
3. Record battery health, SSD health and a full hardware test.
4. Photograph it properly.
5. List it locally at a realistic price.
6. Measure views, messages, offers, negotiation and time-to-sale for 7–14 days.
7. Use that real Dubbo response to adjust the model before buying stock.

The key unanswered question is not whether inexpensive laptops are useful. It is **how quickly clean, well-presented $150–$250 refurbished laptops sell locally at acceptable margins**.

That is best answered with a small real-world demand test.
