## What the new research changes

The existing refurb-market research correctly says the biggest missing proof is real local sale-through and labour economics.

The circular-economy research adds three useful facts:

1. **Dubbo has a genuine repair market**, but prices are not consistently published.
2. A Repair Café changes only the labour/diagnostic side of the economics; it does not magically make expensive parts or obsolete hardware viable.
3. There is a potentially significant stream of reusable/repairable material in bulky/e-waste systems, but no Dubbo condition audit exists yet. Do not assume discarded equipment is good stock.

## Current Dubbo repair-price evidence

### Bicycle benchmark
Wheeler Cycles currently publishes:
- Level 1 service: **$130 + parts**
- Level 2 service: **$190 + parts**

Source:
https://www.wheelercycles.com.au/workshop

This is useful beyond bikes. It demonstrates how quickly skilled labour/service can approach the replacement value of a cheap product.

### Computer benchmark
Right Click Go advertises:
- hardware/software repair;
- upgrades;
- backup/recovery;
- **No Fix, No Pay**.

It does not publish a simple public dollar rate.

Source:
https://www.rightclickgo.com.au/pricing/

### Phone/tablet benchmark
Case Indulgence's current Dubbo pages use model-specific assessment and quotes rather than one public rate. Their repair-vs-replace framing considers:
- device age;
- software support;
- whether the fault is isolated;
- multiple failures;
- frame/secondary damage;
- part availability;
- equivalent replacement;
- accessories/setup/data value.

Sources:
https://caseindulgence.com.au/phone-repair-dubbo/
https://caseindulgence.com.au/tablet-repair-dubbo/

## Better economic decision rule

For customer-owned repair:

**repair total**
= diagnosis/call-out + labour + parts + travel + expected repeat-risk

Compare against:

**replacement-equivalent cost**
= equivalent replacement + setup/data migration + accessories + disposal friction - salvage/resale value.

For refurb stock:

**all-in stock cost**
= acquisition + inbound freight + parts + consumables + labour value + failed-unit allowance + marketplace/transaction costs + returns allowance.

The existing project's $80–$100 visible gross-margin buffer remains a reasonable **decision buffer**, not a proven Dubbo profitability rate.

## What is still missing

No current Dubbo dataset gives:
- median computer diagnostic fee;
- hourly repair labour;
- common-fault quote distribution;
- repair approval/rejection rate;
- percentage technically repairable but uneconomic;
- average part lead time;
- repeat/rework percentage;
- resale time-to-sale for $150–$250 refurbished laptops.

Therefore the existing market concept remains a **testable hypothesis**, not a validated business.

## Highest-value measurement

Collect 30–50 real repair/refurb observations:

- device/model/age;
- fault;
- diagnostic time;
- quoted labour;
- parts;
- replacement equivalent;
- repair approved? yes/no;
- reason declined;
- actual repair time;
- successful?
- sale/listing price if refurb;
- achieved price;
- days to sell;
- return/rework.

This would build the first useful Dubbo repair-economics curve.

## Repair Café implication

Australian Repair Cafés frequently report strong overall fix rates, but electronics/computers are harder than textiles and simple mechanical repairs. The Productivity Commission's Right to Repair report cites a Repair Café International laptop/computer success rate around 45% in the evidence it reviewed.

A Dubbo Repair Café should therefore initially be treated as:
- diagnosis;
- simple fixes/upgrades;
- education;
- parts identification;
- commercial referral;
- “not worth repairing” evidence collection;

not as a promise that every dead laptop can be economically revived.

Sources:
https://assets.pc.gov.au/inquiries/completed/repair/report/repair.pdf
https://dashboard.repairmonitor.org/?cafe=all&country=au&language=en&year=all

## Sourcing warning

The 2023 SSROC bulky-waste audit found about 58% by weight recyclable or potentially reusable in its NSW metropolitan sample. That is evidence that bulky-waste reuse pilots can uncover value, but **it is not a Dubbo stock-quality estimate**.

Source:
https://ssroc.nsw.gov.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/2023-Regional-Report-Bulky-Waste-Audit-clean-up-FINAL_de-identified-for-publication.pdf

Before buying or accepting bulk “e-waste”, run a controlled sample and classify:
- sale-ready;
- minor repair;
- major repair;
- parts donor;
- recycling only.

That sample matters more to the business model than a generic national e-waste growth statistic.
