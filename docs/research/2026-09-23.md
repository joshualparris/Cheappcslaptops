# $100 PC Challenge - Dubbo NSW

**Research snapshot:** 23 September 2026  
**Hard budget:** AUD $100 maximum, including item price, advertised shipping, mandatory fees and essential missing parts.  
**Destination:** Dubbo NSW 2830.  

## Executive answer

The realistic upper end of the current market is a **4th-generation Intel Core i5/i7 ex-office desktop with 8GB RAM**. At the time of research, two standout eBay listings sat exactly on the cap with advertised free delivery:

1. **HP ProDesk 600 G1 SFF - Core i5-4590 / 8GB / 128GB SSD / Windows 11 Pro - $99**.
2. **Dell OptiPlex 9020 SFF - Core i7-4770 / 8GB / 500GB HDD - $99**.

The HP is the better complete everyday computer because the SSD matters immediately. The Dell is the stronger CPU platform if an SSD can be added later outside the challenge.

A 7th-generation Dell OptiPlex 7050 Micro with i5-7500T, 4GB RAM and 128GB SSD was sitting at **$53.36 + $20.25 postage = $73.61 observed**, but it was an auction, so that is **not a confirmed purchase price**.

No complete dedicated-GPU gaming PC at or below $100 all-in was verified during this public-web sweep. A current Gumtree machine with FX-6300, GTX 1050 2GB, 16GB RAM, 120GB SSD and 400GB HDD was **$150 firm, pickup only**, which is a useful marker for where genuine entry gaming starts in the visible market.

## Why a build-from-parts approach loses at $100

The research repeatedly hit the same economics:

1. A useful used GPU can consume half or more of the entire budget.
2. A cheap barebone often needs RAM + storage + power supply/adapter.
3. Postage on several separate listings destroys the budget.
4. SFF office PCs may require low-profile cards and have limited PSU capacity.
5. A complete ex-office PC benefits from one shipping charge and already includes case, board, CPU, cooler, PSU and RAM.

At a $100 hard cap, **buying the best complete ex-office desktop is the rational strategy** unless a local garage-cleanout/Marketplace bundle appears.

## Bottom line

**How powerful can a PC genuinely be for $100 AUD all-in in this snapshot?**

A realistic upper-end specification is:

> **4th-gen Core i5/i7, 8GB DDR3, 128GB SSD or 500GB HDD, Intel HD 4600, complete ex-office SFF chassis.**

That is a **strong basic/older general-purpose desktop**, capable of retro gaming and lighter/older games, but not a modern gaming machine.

The path to a much more exciting $100 gaming result is not retail parts - it is waiting for a badly listed local complete PC, a business clearance, or a seller who undervalues an old dedicated-GPU tower. That is exactly what the planned live version of this project should watch for.
